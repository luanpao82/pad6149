What Is This Assignment?

This is the culmination of the semester. Across the term you built a start-up nonprofit — the Petco Love Lost start-up, jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love to operate the free, AI-powered Petco Love Lost photo-matching database across all nine Volusia County municipalities. You designed its governance, its team and volunteer program, its brand, its financial plan, its fundraising strategy, its strategic plan, its program evaluation, and its advocacy agenda — and you published every piece to a live website.

Now you do two things to finish. First, you make sure the website is complete and public — every page built, polished, and working. Second, you record a 5–8 minute video that pitches the organization and walks a viewer through what you built.

Think of this as the video a funder watches before deciding to take a meeting, a partner watches before signing an MOU, or a community member watches before they register their lost pet. It's the organization's public introduction — and you are its face.

This case is real, and this work travels

This is not a hypothetical that ends with a grade. Both Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love have supported this work with real information and data. After the semester, completed sites are shared with Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, and your work may inform how the Petco Love Lost program is actually operated in Volusia County. Build the final site and record the pitch as if the two partners are your audience — because they are.

You must appear on camera and narrate in your own voice. This is not a slide deck with a voiceover. This is you, the founding Executive Director, personally presenting the organization you built. Stakeholders invest in people, not documents — they need to see and hear the leader.

Beyond that, be creative. Use everything you learned about AI content creation (Week 12), your website, and visual storytelling to make this compelling.

Part 2: Record the Pitch Video

Your video pitches the start-up and shows the work behind it. Use this arc as your spine — the timings are a guide, not a script:

Segment Time What to Cover
Intro — you + the organization 0:00–1:00 Introduce yourself as the founding ED and introduce the start-up: what it is, the partners behind it, and the problem it exists to solve
Walk the site & the deliverables you built 1:00–6:00 Move through the work — governance, team, financials, strategy, programs, advocacy — showing the pages you built and explaining the thinking behind each
Synthesize impact & make the ask 6:00–7:30 Pull it together: what this organization will do for lost pets and families in Volusia County, and what you're asking the viewer to do
Close — URL + contact 7:30–8:00 Website URL and how to reach you, over your brand and closing music

You don't have to hit these times exactly, and you can open with a story or a statistic instead of your name — but by the end, a viewer should understand the organization, have seen the work, and know what you want them to do.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Pitch Video Outline

[0:00–1:00] Intro — you + the organization: Face to camera. "Roughly ten million dogs and cats go missing in the United States every year. I'm [Name], founding director of [your start-up's name] — a nonprofit created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love to make sure that in Volusia County, a lost pet doesn't have to become a shelter statistic. We operate the free, AI-powered Petco Love Lost database across all nine of our municipalities. Upload a photo, and it searches a national pool of lost and found pets in seconds."

[1:00–2:00] The problem & the partners: Narration over data graphics (Canva) and a short screen recording of your About Us page. Halifax Humane Society shelters, treats, and rehomes nearly 15,000 animals a year; Petco Love Lost is already used by more than 3,000 shelters, with over 300,000 pets registered and more than 100,000 reunions to date. Quote Halifax's CEO: the technology "can help reunite lost pets within minutes — without them ever having to enter a shelter."

[2:00–3:00] Governance & team: Screen recording of your Leadership and Team pages. Explain how the board balances the two founding partners with independent community and technical voices, and how staff and volunteers keep the database working countywide.

[3:00–4:00] Financials & strategy: Show your Financials and Strategy pages. Budget overview, revenue diversification, and the strategic priorities for the first three years. "Our funding is diversified by design — no single source carries the whole program."

[4:00–5:15] Programs & impact: Show your Programs page. How the Petco Love Lost program runs, and how you measure success — reunions, pets registered, days-to-reunion, shelter intake avoided.

[5:15–6:00] Advocacy: Show your Advocacy page. The case for countywide adoption across all nine municipalities and the policy steps to get there.

[6:00–7:30] Synthesize & make the ask: Face to camera, personal and direct. "Every reunion is a family made whole and one less animal in a shelter. That's what your support builds. If you believe no pet in Volusia County should be lost for lack of a photo match, partner with us, register your organization, or give."

[7:30–8:00] Close: Brand card, website URL, contact info, closing music.

Creative Approaches

This is not a PowerPoint read aloud. Some approaches that work well for this case:

Website walkthrough + face camera: Alternate between screen recordings of the pages you built and face-to-camera segments explaining the strategy behind each one. This is the most natural fit for the arc above.

Walk-and-talk: Film yourself somewhere relevant to the mission — outside an animal shelter, a Volusia County park, your neighborhood — and narrate as you go. Cut between your narration and supporting visuals.

Documentary style: Open with the problem (the ten-million-missing-pets statistic, a lost-pet story, or news coverage of the countywide deployment), introduce the start-up as the response, and close with your vision.

Interview format: Have someone interview you about the organization, or answer questions as if in a funder or partner meeting.

Use AI-generated images and short clips (from Week 12) as B-roll — visuals that play while you narrate. Mix face-to-camera segments with supporting visuals for a professional result.

Technical Requirements

Recording

  • Your face and voice must appear in the video. Screen recordings, slideshows, and AI-generated narration alone do not meet the requirement.
  • Length: 5–8 minutes. Under 5 is too rushed; over 8 loses the audience.
  • Quality: You don't need professional equipment. A smartphone in a well-lit, quiet space is enough. Use a tripod or prop your phone at eye level.

Recording Tips

  • Lighting: Face a window for natural light. Avoid overhead-only lighting (it creates shadows).
  • Audio: Record in a quiet space. If using a phone, stay within 2–3 feet of the microphone. Earbuds with a built-in mic improve audio quality significantly.
  • Framing: Head and shoulders in frame, eyes at camera level. Leave a small space above your head.
  • Background: Clean and uncluttered — or a setting relevant to the mission (a park, a shelter exterior, your neighborhood).
  • Screen recording: Record your website walkthrough separately (Mac: Cmd+Shift+5; Windows: Xbox Game Bar, Win+G; iPhone: Screen Recording in Control Center), then cut it into your video during editing.

Editing

You don't need advanced editing skills. These free tools handle the basics:

Tool What It Does How to Use It
CapCut Free video editor — trim, cut, add text, transitions, music Import your clips → arrange on the timeline → add text overlays and transitions → export as MP4
Descript AI-powered editor — edit video by editing text Upload your recording → Descript transcribes it → delete words in the transcript to cut the video; Studio Sound cleans up audio; free tier available
Canva Video Combine clips with graphics, title cards, B-roll Upload your recordings → add branded title cards, data graphics, and AI images between segments
iMovie (Mac/iPhone) Apple's free video editor Combine clips, add transitions and music, export directly to YouTube
YouTube Studio Basic editing + captions after upload Trim start/end, add end screens, auto-generate captions (then review)

AI-Enhanced Editing with Descript

Descript is especially useful here. It transcribes your video and lets you edit by deleting text — remove "ums," cut rambling sections, and tighten your delivery without learning traditional video editing. Its Studio Sound feature (free tier) reduces background noise and improves voice clarity, which helps if you can't record in an ideal space.

Captions (do not skip)

Captions make your video accessible and let it play silently in a feed — the way most people first encounter a nonprofit video. Add them:

  • YouTube Studio: Subtitles → auto-generate → review and correct (auto-captions mangle names — fix your organization's name, "Petco Love Lost," "Halifax Humane Society," and "Volusia County").
  • CapCut / Descript / Canva: Auto-caption on the timeline, then correct the same terms and burn them in.

Upload to YouTube

  1. Go to YouTube Studio (studio.youtube.com) → "Create" → "Upload videos"
  2. Upload your MP4 file
  3. Title: [Your Start-Up Name] — Petco Love Lost in Volusia County
  4. Description: your organization name, mission, and website URL
  5. Visibility: Unlisted (only people with the link) or Public — your choice
  6. Copy the URL → submit to Webcourses

No YouTube account? A shared video file (Google Drive or OneDrive link set to "anyone with the link can view") is acceptable — just confirm the link works in a private browser window before you submit.

Add to Your Website

Embed or link the video on your Home page — the first thing a visitor sees. In Google Sites: Insert → YouTube → paste your URL. In Wix: Add → Video → YouTube.

This is the final piece of the site. After this, your Home page has a mission statement, a hero image, and a video of the founding director presenting the organization. That's a real nonprofit homepage.

Copy-Paste Prompt: Draft Your Pitch Script

Paste this into ChatGPT or Claude:

I'm the founding executive director of a start-up nonprofit created by
Halifax Humane Society (a Daytona Beach shelter that rehomes ~15,000
animals a year across nine Volusia County municipalities) and Petco
Love (which runs the free, AI photo-matching Petco Love Lost database —
3,000+ shelters, 300,000+ pets registered, 100,000+ reunions). Our
single mission is running Petco Love Lost well across all of Volusia
County. Roughly 10 million pets go missing in the U.S. each year.

Write a 6-minute spoken pitch script (about 850 words) that (1) opens
with a hook, (2) introduces me and the organization, (3) walks through
the governance, team, financials, strategy, programs, and advocacy work
I built, (4) synthesizes the impact, and (5) closes with a clear ask and
our website URL. Conversational, first person, for camera — not a slide
deck. Leave [brackets] where I insert my own specifics.

Then rewrite it in your own words so it sounds like you, not the model.

Copy-Paste Prompt: Tighten and Time Your Script

Once you have a draft you like, paste it back with:

Here is my pitch script: [paste]. It needs to fit in 5–8 minutes spoken
at a natural pace. Cut filler, flag any sentence that's hard to say out
loud, and tell me roughly how long each section runs. Then suggest one
stronger opening line and one stronger closing ask specific to reuniting
lost pets with families in Volusia County.

The judgment stays yours — the model trims and times, you decide what stays.

What Makes a Great Pitch Video?

Do Don't
Open with something compelling — the 10-million statistic, a lost-pet story, a question Open with "Hi, my name is... and for my class project..."
Speak naturally — conversational, not scripted-robotic Read a script word-for-word while staring at a paper
Show passion for the mission — this should feel personal Present like a detached book report
Show the actual pages you built — governance, financials, programs, advocacy Talk to the camera for 8 minutes with nothing else on screen
Add corrected captions so it plays silently and accessibly Ship auto-captions that spell your org's name wrong
End with a clear ask and your website URL End by trailing off or saying "that's it"
Keep it under 8 minutes — tighter is better Ramble past 10 minutes because you didn't edit
