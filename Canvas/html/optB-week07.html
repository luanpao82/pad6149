What Is This Assignment?

Your start-up has a mission, governance, and a team. Now the question every funder — including your founding partners — will ask: how much does it cost to run Petco Love Lost countywide, and where does the money come from?

Remember what your organization actually does. Petco Love Lost is a free, AI-powered photo-matching database — upload a photo and it searches a national pool of lost and found pets in seconds. The platform is provided by Petco Love; it costs your start-up nothing to use. What does cost money is running it well on the ground across all nine Volusia County municipalities: getting pets registered, coordinating with Halifax Humane Society (which shelters nearly 15,000 animals a year), staffing the outreach, and keeping the local data current. Your budget is about the countywide deployment, not a software license.

You submit your budget and narrative to Webcourses. This budget becomes a core component of your Strategic Plan (Week 9), so treat it with that weight.

In PAD 6208, you analyzed an existing nonprofit's financial reports and completed a budget assignment. Now you're adapting that work to build a budget from zero for the Petco Love Lost start-up.

What Do I Submit?

1. Revenue Projections (Year 1)

Identify your revenue sources and estimate amounts. This start-up has one unusual advantage most new nonprofits don't: two founding partners — Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love — who have a stake in the program succeeding. That likely means meaningful seed support. But be realistic and diversify: over-reliance on a single founding funder is exactly the kind of concentration risk your PAD 6208 analysis flagged.

Look up comparable animal-welfare organizations on GuideStar / Candid (candid.org) — their Form 990s show actual revenue breakdowns by source. Use Candid's Foundation Directory to research foundations that fund animal welfare, community services, or technology-for-good in Central Florida, including average grant sizes and deadlines — access it free through the UCF Library databases (search "Foundation Directory Online" in the library's database list). The Nonprofit Finance Fund (nff.org) also offers free startup-specific budgeting guides and cash flow templates.

AI Tip: Budget Template

Use ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini to generate a starting framework:

Create a Year 1 operating budget for a start-up nonprofit that runs
Petco Love Lost — a free, AI-powered lost-pet photo-matching database —
countywide across nine Volusia County, Florida municipalities in
partnership with Halifax Humane Society (which shelters ~15,000 animals
a year). Annual budget: $275,000. Include 4 staff positions with salary
ranges, local technology/registration infrastructure (scanners, photo
stations, tablets), outreach and registration drives, occupancy,
insurance, and contingency. Note that the Petco Love Lost platform
itself is free to use. Format as a table with amount and percentage
columns.

Use the output as a starting framework, then adjust based on your own staffing model and local cost data (check Indeed or Idealist for Volusia County salary benchmarks).

Don't let the AI invent a platform fee

A common AI mistake on this case: the tool assumes you have to pay for the lost-pet database and adds a large software line item. You don't — Petco Love Lost is free, used by more than 3,000 shelters nationwide. Your technology costs are local hardware (scanners, photo stations, tablets) and your own website. Correct the output when the tool gets this wrong; that judgment is your job.

3. Budget Narrative (Brief)

Write 1 page explaining your budget rationale: - Why did you allocate resources this way? - What are the biggest financial risks in Year 1? (What happens when the Petco Love founding grant tapers? What if registration drives cost more per pet than projected?) - What assumptions are you making about municipal buy-in and volunteer capacity? - How does this budget support the strategic goals you'll articulate in Week 9?

Website Update

Add a "Financials" page to your website. Funders and donors increasingly expect financial transparency from nonprofits — even brand-new ones — and both of your founding partners will want to see that the deployment is being run responsibly. Getting this on your site now builds credibility from day one.

Your page should include: - A summary of your projected Year 1 budget — use Canva to create a clean pie chart or infographic that's more visually engaging than a raw spreadsheet - A statement about financial accountability and stewardship of partner and donor funds - Link to a downloadable budget summary (PDF)
