What Is This Assignment?

You have a strategic plan with goals and objectives (Week 9). Now the question every funder, board member, and partner will ask: how do you know it's working?

For the Petco Love Lost start-up, this question has real weight. Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love are launching the first coordinated countywide deployment of a free, AI-powered photo-matching database across Volusia County. They will want evidence that the model works — not just that pets are registered, but that lost pets are actually getting home, faster, and that fewer animals are entering the shelter system in the first place. Your evaluation framework is how that evidence gets built.

In PAD 6327, you developed a Program Evaluation Plan for an existing nonprofit — designing evaluation questions, selecting methods, identifying data sources, and proposing an analysis framework. Now you're designing evaluation from the ground up for a start-up, with the advantage of building measurement in from day one instead of retrofitting it later. Most nonprofits struggle with evaluation because they design programs first and think about measurement after. You're doing it the right way — defining what success looks like before the program scales.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up

You are designing the evaluation framework for a start-up nonprofit jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love to run the Petco Love Lost program in Volusia County, Florida. The organization is fictional and yours to design; the partners, the problem, and the data are real. Roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing in the United States each year. Petco Love Lost is a free, AI-powered photo-matching database — upload a photo and it searches a national pool of lost and found pets in seconds — now used by more than 3,000 shelters and rescues, with over 300,000 pets registered and more than 100,000 reunions to date. Halifax Humane Society shelters, treats, and rehomes nearly 15,000 animals a year across nine Volusia County municipalities. Because this is a real partnership, your completed work may inform how the program is actually operated on the ground.

What Do I Submit?

1. Program Descriptions (1–2 pages)

Before you can evaluate, you need to clearly define what you're evaluating. Describe your organization's 2–3 core programs or service areas. For each program, include:

  • Program name
  • Target population for this specific program
  • Activities and services provided
  • Expected outputs (what you produce — pets registered, shelters onboarded, outreach events held)
  • Expected outcomes (what changes — reunions, faster time-to-reunion, shelter intake avoided, community awareness)

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Program 1

Program: Lost Pet Registry & AI Photo-Matching

Target population: Volusia County residents who have lost or found a dog or cat, plus the animal-control and shelter staff who intake stray animals across the nine municipalities.

Activities: - Register found and lost pets into the free Petco Love Lost photo database - Run AI photo-matching to surface likely matches within seconds - Support field reunions so a found pet can be returned to its owner directly — "without ever having to enter a shelter" - Provide a bilingual (English/Spanish) help line and simple mobile upload workflow

Expected outputs (Year 1): - 8,000 Volusia County pets registered in the database - 4,000 photo-match searches run by residents and partner shelters - 90% of registrations completed with a usable, matchable photo

Expected outcomes: - A measurable share of found pets reunited before intake ("without ever entering a shelter") - Reduced median time-to-reunion compared to the pre-program return-to-owner process - Reduced shelter intake of stray-but-owned animals at Halifax Humane Society

These figures are one plausible Year 1 target set, not fixed facts — you set your own based on your strategic plan and county scale.

2. Logic Model (1 page)

Create a logic model for your primary program. A logic model maps the relationship between resources, activities, outputs, and outcomes — it's the visual blueprint for how your program creates change.

The logic model makes visible the theory of change: register pets → AI matches → faster reunions → fewer animals entering shelters. Each link in this chain needs to be measured.

3. Evaluation Questions (half a page)

Define 3–5 evaluation questions that your framework is designed to answer. Good evaluation questions are: - Directly tied to your strategic goals (Week 9) - Answerable with data you can realistically collect - Useful for decision-making — the answers should tell you what to do next

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Evaluation Questions

  1. Is registration reaching pet owners across all nine Volusia County municipalities, or are some communities under-represented?
  2. Of the pets registered as lost or found, how many are reunited, and how does the AI photo-matching contribute to those reunions?
  3. Is the program reducing time-to-reunion and enabling reunions "without ever having to enter a shelter"?
  4. Is stray intake at Halifax Humane Society declining for animals that are owned but lost, and can that decline be attributed to the program?
  5. Are partner shelters and municipal animal-control units actually adopting and using the tool as part of their intake workflow?

4. Data Collection Plan (1–2 pages)

For each evaluation question, describe: - What data you'll collect (quantitative, qualitative, or both) - How you'll collect it (platform analytics, shelter administrative records, surveys, interviews, partner logs) - When you'll collect it (at registration, at reunion, monthly, quarterly, annually) - Who is responsible for data collection

Be realistic about capacity. A small start-up cannot run a randomized controlled trial. Lean on data the program already generates — the Petco Love Lost platform, Halifax Humane Society's intake system, and partner-shelter logs — and add lightweight surveys only where the administrative data can't answer the question.

5. Analysis and Reporting Plan (half a page)

How will you make sense of the data and communicate findings?

  • How often will you analyze and review data? (quarterly is standard for a start-up)
  • Who reviews the findings? (ED, board, program staff, and the Halifax/Petco Love partners)
  • How will findings be shared? (board reports, partner reports, annual impact report, website updates)
  • How will evaluation results inform program improvements? (describe a feedback loop)

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Reporting Cycle

  • Monthly: Program Director pulls registration and match data from the platform and reviews reach by municipality in Google Sheets.
  • Quarterly: Program Director compiles a 2-page dashboard for the board and the Halifax/Petco Love partners (pets registered, reunions, median time-to-reunion, stray-intake trend, municipalities active).
  • Annually: ED produces a public impact report summarizing Year 1 outcomes — published on the website and shared with donors, partners, and the nine municipalities.
  • Continuous improvement: Quarterly meetings review the data and identify adjustments. If a municipality shows low registration, the team targets outreach there; if match-to-reunion conversion is low, the team reviews photo quality and reunion follow-up.

6. Ethical Considerations (half a page)

Address evaluation ethics specific to this program: - How will you protect the personal information of residents who register pets (contact details, home location)? - How will you obtain consent to use registration and reunion data for evaluation and reporting? - What data-sharing agreement is needed between the start-up, Halifax Humane Society, and Petco Love so intake records can be used without exposing individuals? - How will you ensure evaluation and outreach reach all nine municipalities equitably, including lower-income and non-English-speaking residents?

Use Perplexity.ai to research good practice: "What are the data privacy and consent considerations for a nonprofit that collects pet-owner contact information and location data through an online lost-and-found registry, and shares outcome data with partner shelters?"
