What Is This Assignment?

This is where your version of the start-up gets its face and its voice.

Since Week 3 you've been building a website for a new nonprofit start-up jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love — a single-purpose organization whose only job is to run the Petco Love Lost program well across Volusia County. So far it has lived under a working name, a placeholder logo, and whatever colors you grabbed quickly to get the site up.

This week you lock in the fundamentals and make them yours: organization name, mission, vision, tagline, brand voice, logo, color palette, and typography. Then you apply that identity consistently across the whole site.

What's fixed vs. what's yours

The case is real and fixed. The partners (Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love), the problem (lost pets in Volusia County), and the tool (a free, AI-powered photo-matching database) do not change from student to student. What is yours — and what you're graded on this week — is the brand identity you invent to represent this start-up: the name you give it, the way it looks, and the way it sounds. Two students working the same case should produce two clearly different brands, both of which fit the mission.

Everything you finalize this week should be reflected in a visual refresh of your website by the end of the week.

What Do I Submit?

1. Organization Name — Named and Verified

The Petco Love Lost program has a name. Your start-up does not — you name it. Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love created the entity; your job is to give the Volusia County operation a distinct identity of its own. It might lean on the place (Volusia, Daytona, Halifax River), the mission (reunite, find, home, lost & found), or the animals themselves — but it should read as a real, registrable nonprofit, not a program label.

Once you have a name you like, confirm it's legally available and registrable. A collision with an existing entity can block incorporation and trademark later.

How to verify your name:

Check Where What You're Looking For
Florida business name Sunbiz.org → Search by Name No active corporation, LLC, or nonprofit with the same or confusingly similar name
Federal trademark USPTO Trademark Search No registered or pending trademark for animal-welfare / rescue services
Domain availability Namecheap or any registrar Check whether a .org domain is available (you don't need to buy it yet)
Social media handles Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn Check whether your preferred handle is free on major platforms
IRS EIN database IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search No existing 501(c)(3) with the same name in Florida

Don't collide with the partners

Your start-up is powered by Petco Love Lost, but it is not Petco, Petco Love, the Petco Foundation, or Halifax Humane Society. Do not adopt a name, logo, or wordmark that could be mistaken for either partner's brand — that's a trademark problem, not a homage. Reference the partnership in your About copy, not in your name.

Try Namelix (namelix.com) — an AI name generator that returns name ideas with matching logo concepts from your keywords. Or prompt a chatbot directly:

registrable names. Avoid using "Petco," "Halifax Humane," or
"Foundation." Lean warm, local, and hopeful. For each, give a

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Name Verification

(One plausible version — invent your own.) - Working name chosen: "Volusia Pet Reunite" - Sunbiz.org search: No active entity named "Volusia Pet Reunite" in Florida ✓ - USPTO search: No registered trademark for the mark in animal-welfare services ✓ - Domain: volusiapetreunite.org — available ✓ - Social: @volusiapetreunite available on Instagram and Facebook ✓ - IRS EOS: No existing 501(c)(3) with this name in Florida ✓

Name confirmed. Alternatives considered if it had been taken: "Home Again Volusia" and "FoundList Daytona."

Once verified, this becomes your official name across all documents and your website.

2. Mission Statement

Your mission says, in one sentence, what this organization does and for whom. For this case, that's narrow by design: one program, one county. A strong nonprofit mission statement is:

  • Concise — under 25 words if possible
  • Action-oriented — starts with a verb or clearly states what you do
  • Specific — names who you serve and the change you create
  • Memorable — someone can repeat it after hearing it once

AI Tip: Mission Statement Refinement

Upload your Week 3 About Us copy, your Week 4 governance work, and your Week 5 HR/volunteer plan to NotebookLM, then ask:

Based on everything I've written about this organization, help me
write a mission statement for a Volusia County nonprofit that runs
the Petco Love Lost program — a free, AI-powered photo-matching
database that reunites lost pets with their families, often before
the animal ever enters a shelter. Keep it under 25 words,
action-oriented, and specific about who we serve. Give me 5 options.

Pick the one that resonates, or combine elements from several.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Mission Refinement

Draft (Week 3): "We use the Petco Love Lost photo-matching database to help people in Volusia County find their lost pets and reduce the number of animals entering the shelter system."

Final (Week 6): "Volusia Pet Reunite uses free AI photo-matching to bring lost dogs and cats home across Volusia County — before they ever enter a shelter."

Shorter, sharper, same substance. The full detail lives on the About page — the mission is the headline.

3. Vision Statement

Your mission says what you do. Your vision describes what Volusia County looks like when you've succeeded. It's aspirational — the future you're working toward, not your day-to-day activities.

A strong vision statement is: - Future-oriented — describes a changed reality, not your program - Inspiring — motivates staff, volunteers, donors, and pet owners - Ambitious but credible — aspirational without being vague or grandiose

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Vision Statement

Mission: "Volusia Pet Reunite uses free AI photo-matching to bring lost dogs and cats home across Volusia County — before they ever enter a shelter."

Vision: "A Volusia County where no family stays separated from a lost pet — and no found animal waits in a shelter for an owner who is searching for them."

Nationally, roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing every year. The vision reframes that number at the county scale: a home where the gap between lost and found closes to near zero.

4. Tagline

A tagline is the short phrase that sits beside your logo. It captures the spirit of the organization in a few words.

Examples from real nonprofits: - Habitat for Humanity: "Building strength, stability and self-reliance" - United Way: "Live United" - Doctors Without Borders: "Medical aid where it's needed most"

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Tagline

"Lost. Found. Home." — three words that trace the whole journey your program shortens from weeks to minutes.

5. Brand Voice

Your voice is how the organization sounds in writing — the tone of your website, your social posts, and the message a worried pet owner reads at 2 a.m. Decide it deliberately now so every page is consistent.

Pick 3–4 voice traits and one sentence on what each means in practice. Then define what you avoid.

AI Tip: Define Your Voice, Then Enforce It

Help me define a brand voice for Volusia Pet Reunite, a Volusia County
nonprofit that reunites people with lost pets using free AI
photo-matching. Our audiences are (1) anxious owners who just lost a
pet, (2) donors, and (3) volunteers. Propose 4 voice traits with a
one-line definition each, plus 3 "we don't sound like this" examples.
Then rewrite this sentence in that voice: "Upload a photo of your
missing pet to search our database."

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Brand Voice

  • Reassuring — we speak first to fear ("Losing a pet is terrifying. Start here."), not to features.
  • Plain-spoken — no jargon; a stressed owner can act in one read.
  • Hopeful, not hype — we cite real reunions, we don't over-promise a match.
  • Local — we name Volusia towns and Halifax Humane Society; we sound like a neighbor.
  • We avoid: corporate press-release tone, guilt-based donor appeals, and "revolutionary technology" language.

6. Logo (Final Version)

If you made a quick logo in Week 3, refine it now into something professional. Your logo should: - Work at small sizes (a social profile picture) and large sizes (a banner) - Stay readable in black and white - Reflect the mission (reunion, home, animals) without being overly literal - Not resemble the Petco or Halifax Humane Society marks

AI Tip: Logo Design with Canva or Looka

In Canva, try Magic Design or Text to Image; Looka (looka.com) generates full logo suites from a name and a few style choices.

Create a minimalist, warm logo for "Volusia Pet Reunite," a Florida
nonprofit that reunites lost dogs and cats with their families using
AI photo-matching. Style: modern, hopeful, trustworthy. Incorporate a
subtle motif of home, reunion, or a paw — not a corporate tech look.
Primary color: warm teal. Provide a horizontal lockup and a compact
icon-only version.

Download in PNG (transparent background) and SVG if available, and export the icon-only version for social profiles and the site favicon.

7. Brand Color Palette

Define 3–4 colors and their hex codes so your brand stays consistent across website, documents, and slides: - Primary — your dominant color (headers, buttons, logo) - Secondary — accent (highlights, calls to action) - Neutral(s) — background and body-text colors

Use Coolors.co to build a harmonious palette from your primary color, then run every text/background pairing through the WebAIM Contrast Checker to confirm it meets WCAG AA (minimum 4.5:1 contrast). A pet-owner in distress on a phone screen needs to read your site.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Color Palette

Role Color Hex Code
Primary Halifax Teal #0F766E
Secondary Reunion Coral #F97362
Background Warm Off-White #FAFAF7
Text Deep Charcoal #1C1917

Teal for calm and trust, coral for the hopeful "found" moment. Both pass AA against the off-white background.

8. Typography

Type is part of your brand. Pick a heading font and a body font that pair well and are free to use on the web.

  • Google Fonts — free, web-ready fonts you can drop straight into your site.
  • Fontjoy — AI-generated heading + body pairings; enter one font you like and it suggests complements.

Choose fonts that match your voice: this case wants something legible and approachable (a worried owner scanning quickly), not decorative. Record your choices — family names, and which is used for headings vs. body — in your Brand Identity Guide.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Typography

  • Headings: Poppins (friendly, rounded, confident)
  • Body: Inter (highly legible on phones at small sizes)
  • Rationale: warm without being casual; both are free Google Fonts and load fast on a shelter's low-bandwidth kiosk.

9. Target Audiences

Define 3–4 distinct audiences. Each needs different information and a different message:

Audience What They Need to Know How You Reach Them
Pet owners (lost a pet) How to search the database right now, that it's free, that it works fast Website "Report a Lost Pet" flow, social media, vet-office flyers, animal-control referrals
Finders / Good Samaritans How to post a found animal instead of surrendering it to a shelter Website "Found a Pet" flow, neighborhood apps, Halifax Humane intake desk
Donors / Funders Your reunion outcomes, your credibility, how their gift keeps the tool free Website, grant applications, impact reports, social media
Volunteers & partner shelters How to help register animals and spread the word "Get Involved" page, the nine Volusia municipalities' shelters and rescues

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Audience Insight

A key insight: your two most urgent audiences are in opposite emotional states — an owner in panic and a finder feeling helpful — but they need to meet in the same database. The homepage has to serve both in the first screen: one clear path to "I lost a pet," one clear path to "I found a pet." Everything else (donors, volunteers, partners) can live a scroll down. Petco Love Lost already connects more than 3,000 shelters and rescues; your job is to make Volusia County's two paths feel effortless.
