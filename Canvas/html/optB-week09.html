What Is This Assignment?

This is the integration assignment. Everything you've built over the past several weeks — governance, staffing, brand identity, budget, fundraising — now comes together into a cohesive organizational strategy for the Petco Love Lost start-up.

In PAD 6335, you developed a Strategic Plan for an existing nonprofit. You analyzed its environment, assessed strengths and weaknesses, defined strategic goals, and proposed action plans. Now you're doing the same thing — but for the start-up you've been building all semester: a new nonprofit, jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, whose single purpose is to run the Petco Love Lost program well across Volusia County, Florida.

The difference: when you wrote a strategic plan for someone else's organization, you worked with historical data, existing programs, and established stakeholders. Here, you're projecting forward from a launch point. Your start-up is not starting from nothing — it inherits a proven national tool (a free, AI-powered photo-matching database now used by more than 3,000 shelters and rescues, with over 300,000 pets registered and more than 100,000 reunions to date) and a strong local anchor (Halifax, which shelters, treats, and rehomes nearly 15,000 animals a year and serves nine Volusia County municipalities). Your strategic job is to turn that inheritance into the first coordinated countywide deployment — and to plan how it scales, sustains, and reunites lost pets faster than a shelter intake ever could.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up

The problem. Roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing in the United States each year. Most enter the shelter system before they are reunited with their families — if they are reunited at all.

The tool (real). Petco Love Lost is a free, AI-powered photo-matching database operated by Petco Love. Anyone can upload a photo of a lost or found pet, and it searches a national pool in seconds. As Halifax's CEO puts it, the technology "can help reunite lost pets within minutes — without them ever having to enter a shelter."

Your start-up (you design it). A new nonprofit built by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love to operate this program well in Volusia County. You named and branded it in Week 6. This strategic plan is how it wins.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Executive Summary

The numbers below are one plausible version — your own budget and staffing choices from Weeks 5 and 7 should drive your figures.

Our start-up exists to make Volusia County the place where lost pets get home fastest. Roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing nationwide each year, and most enter a shelter before they are reunited — if they are reunited at all. Petco Love Lost, a free, AI-powered photo-matching database, changes that equation: a photo can reunite a pet within minutes, without it ever entering a shelter. Our mission — to reunite every lost pet in Volusia County with its family, quickly and without a shelter stay — guides every part of this strategy.

In Year 1, we will focus on three strategic priorities: (1) launching the first coordinated countywide deployment of Petco Love Lost across all nine Volusia County municipalities, in partnership with Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love; (2) driving adoption — registrations, found-pet uploads, and confirmed reunions — through animal control, veterinarians, and residents; and (3) building a sustainable funding base so the deployment outlives its launch grants. This plan was developed through analysis of the local animal-welfare landscape, real data provided by Halifax and Petco Love, and strategic planning frameworks from the MNM curriculum.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — SWOT (excerpt)

  • Strengths: A proven national tool (3,000+ shelters, 300,000+ pets registered, 100,000+ reunions) inherited at no cost; a credible local anchor in Halifax, which already touches nearly 15,000 animals a year across nine municipalities; the backing of two established partners.
  • Weaknesses: No independent track record or brand recognition as a stand-alone entity; dependence on partners for data, staffing, and legitimacy; reliance on residents actually uploading photos for the tool to work.
  • Opportunities: Being the first coordinated countywide deployment — a model other Florida counties could replicate; reducing shelter intake and its costs; enlisting veterinarians and animal control as registration channels.
  • Threats: Uneven adoption across the nine municipalities; grant funding that expires before the deployment is self-sustaining; public confusion between the start-up, Halifax, and Petco Love.

Competitive / Ecosystem Landscape

This isn't about competition in the business sense — it's about understanding where your organization fits in Volusia County's animal-welfare ecosystem. - Who else operates in this space (municipal animal control agencies, rescue groups, veterinary clinics, neighborhood "lost pet" Facebook groups, Nextdoor)? - What gap does a coordinated, county-wide Petco Love Lost deployment fill that these fragmented channels do not? - How will you collaborate with — rather than duplicate — Halifax's existing shelter operations and each municipality's animal control?

Use Candid / GuideStar (candid.org) to research comparable animal-welfare organizations' programs, budgets, and service areas.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Ecosystem Landscape

Lost-pet recovery in Volusia County today is fragmented: each of the nine municipalities runs its own animal control, residents scatter across neighborhood Facebook and Nextdoor groups, and Halifax absorbs the animals no one claims. Nothing connects these channels, so a dog found in one city rarely reaches an owner searching in the next.

Our start-up does not replace any of them. Its distinctive contribution is the connective layer: one shared, AI-powered database that animal control, veterinarians, rescues, and residents all feed into, so a match can happen countywide in minutes. We complement Halifax's shelter operations by keeping reunifiable pets out of intake, and we complement municipal animal control by giving every jurisdiction the same tool and the same pool.

5. Scaling & Partnership Strategy (1 page)

This is the strategic heart of a start-up built to grow. Address two things concretely:

Scaling across the nine municipalities. The deployment will not launch in all nine jurisdictions at equal strength on day one. Lay out your rollout logic: - Which municipalities do you launch in first, and on what criteria (intake volume, animal-control readiness, population)? - How does adoption in an early city create a playbook for the next? - What has to be true — training, MOUs, data agreements — before a municipality is "live"?

Partnership strategy with Halifax and Petco Love. Your start-up sits between two established organizations. Define the relationship, not just name it: - What does Halifax provide (shelter access, intake data, local credibility, staff time)? - What does Petco Love provide (the platform, national data pool, launch funding, brand)? - Where does your start-up add value that neither partner delivers alone? - How are decisions made and roles divided so the venture doesn't stall between two parents?

Use Perplexity.ai or Google Scholar to find examples of successful multi-jurisdiction nonprofit deployments and public–nonprofit partnership structures you can adapt.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Scaling Logic

Launch in the three highest-intake municipalities first, where Halifax already has the deepest animal-control relationships; use their first-quarter results (registrations, uploads, reunions) as the proof-of-concept deck that brings the remaining six on board. A municipality goes "live" only once its animal control unit is trained on found-pet uploads and a data-sharing MOU is signed. Halifax supplies shelter access and intake data; Petco Love supplies the platform and launch funding; the start-up owns the connective work neither does today — training, coordination, and countywide measurement.
