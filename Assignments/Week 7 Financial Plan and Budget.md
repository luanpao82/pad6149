## What Is This Assignment?

Your start-up has a mission, governance, and a team. Now the question every funder — including your founding partners — will ask: **how much does it cost to run Petco Love Lost countywide, and where does the money come from?**

Remember what your organization actually does. Petco Love Lost is a **free, AI-powered photo-matching database** — upload a photo and it searches a national pool of lost and found pets in seconds. The platform is provided by Petco Love; it costs your start-up nothing to use. What *does* cost money is running it well on the ground across **all nine Volusia County municipalities**: getting pets registered, coordinating with Halifax Humane Society (which shelters nearly 15,000 animals a year), staffing the outreach, and keeping the local data current. Your budget is about the **countywide deployment**, not a software license.

You submit your budget and narrative to Webcourses. This budget becomes a core component of your **Strategic Plan (Week 9)**, so treat it with that weight.

In PAD 6208, you analyzed an existing nonprofit's financial reports and completed a budget assignment. Now you're adapting that work to build a budget from zero for the Petco Love Lost start-up.

## What Do I Submit?

### 1. Revenue Projections (Year 1)

Identify your revenue sources and estimate amounts. This start-up has one unusual advantage most new nonprofits don't: **two founding partners** — Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love — who have a stake in the program succeeding. That likely means meaningful seed support. But be realistic and diversify: over-reliance on a single founding funder is exactly the kind of concentration risk your PAD 6208 analysis flagged.

Look up comparable animal-welfare organizations on **GuideStar / Candid** (candid.org) — their Form 990s show actual revenue breakdowns by source. Use **Candid's Foundation Directory** to research foundations that fund animal welfare, community services, or technology-for-good in Central Florida, including average grant sizes and deadlines — access it free through the **UCF Library databases** (search "Foundation Directory Online" in the library's database list). The **Nonprofit Finance Fund** (nff.org) also offers free startup-specific budgeting guides and cash flow templates.

Common startup revenue sources for this case:

| Source | Typical role for this start-up | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Founding partner support (Petco Love) | Largest single source in Year 1 | Seed grant to launch the countywide deployment; may taper in later years |
| Founding partner support (Halifax Humane Society) | Cash + in-kind | Shared space, shelter coordination, staff time, existing donor access |
| Foundation grants | Second-largest source | Animal welfare, community, and tech-for-good funders; some fund new organizations |
| Individual & community donations | Steady base | Pet owners, board giving, community fundraising |
| Corporate sponsorships | Local businesses | Veterinary clinics, pet retailers, Petco stores, groomers |
| Municipal contributions | Modest, but signals buy-in | Small contributions/contracts across the 9 municipalities served |
| Events / community fundraising | Low net in Year 1 | Adoption/registration events, builds visibility |

> *One plausible version. These figures are a student's design choices, not fixed facts — yours should reflect your own assumptions and research.*
>
> | Source | Amount | % |
> |---|---|---|
> | Founding grant — Petco Love | $95,000 | 34.5% |
> | Foundation grants (2 grants) | $55,000 | 20.0% |

> | Individual & community donations | $40,000 | 14.5% |

> *Even though Petco Love is a founding partner, no single source exceeds ~35%. That diversification is deliberate — a key lesson from my PAD 6208 analysis, where the organization I studied drew 72% of its revenue from one government contract and was one budget cycle away from collapse.*

### 2. Expense Budget (Year 1)

Your costs follow the work: **staffing** the deployment, **local technology and registration infrastructure** (the Petco Love Lost platform is free, but scanners, photo-capture stations, and tablets are not), **outreach and registration drives** across nine municipalities, and **shelter coordination** with Halifax Humane Society.

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Expenses (Year 1)
> *One plausible version — adjust to your own staffing model and local cost data.*
>
> | Category | Amount | % | Notes |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Personnel** | | | |
> | Program Director | $56,000 | | Runs the countywide deployment; partner liaison |
> | Registration & Outreach Coordinator (FT) | $40,000 | | Plans and staffs registration drives |
> | Community Outreach Associate (FT) | $32,000 | | Field registration, bilingual community events |
> | Data & Technology Steward (PT, 0.5 FTE) | $22,000 | | Keeps local records current; trains partner sites |
> | Benefits & payroll taxes (22%) | $33,000 | | |
> | **Subtotal Personnel** | **$183,000** | **66.5%** | |
> | **Technology & platform stewardship** | $28,000 | 10.2% | Microchip scanners, photo-capture stations, tablets, org website — *not* a platform fee; Petco Love Lost is free |
> | **Outreach & registration drives (9 municipalities)** | $30,000 | 10.9% | Events, multilingual printing, transportation, supplies |
> | **Occupancy** | $9,000 | 3.3% | Shared space at Halifax Humane Society (partly in-kind) |
> | **Insurance** | $6,000 | 2.2% | General liability, D&O |
> | **Marketing & communications** | $8,000 | 2.9% | "Register your pet" campaign, social, print |
> | **Professional development** | $3,000 | 1.1% | Staff training, conferences |
> | **Miscellaneous / contingency** | $8,000 | 2.9% | |
> | **Total** | **$275,000** | **100%** | |
>
> *Personnel sits at ~66% — typical for a service-and-outreach organization whose core work is people getting pets registered face-to-face across a nine-city county. The technology line is real infrastructure (scanners and photo stations at intake and partner sites), not a license: the AI database itself costs nothing to use.*

> [!tip] AI Tip: Budget Template
> Use **ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini** to generate a starting framework:
> ```
> Create a Year 1 operating budget for a start-up nonprofit that runs
> Petco Love Lost — a free, AI-powered lost-pet photo-matching database —
> countywide across nine Volusia County, Florida municipalities in
> partnership with Halifax Humane Society (which shelters ~15,000 animals
> a year). Annual budget: $275,000. Include 4 staff positions with salary
> ranges, local technology/registration infrastructure (scanners, photo
> stations, tablets), outreach and registration drives, occupancy,
> insurance, and contingency. Note that the Petco Love Lost platform
> itself is free to use. Format as a table with amount and percentage
> columns.
> ```
> Use the output as a starting framework, then adjust based on your own staffing model and local cost data (check Indeed or Idealist for Volusia County salary benchmarks).

> [!warning] Don't let the AI invent a platform fee
> A common AI mistake on this case: the tool assumes you have to *pay* for the lost-pet database and adds a large software line item. You don't — Petco Love Lost is free, used by more than 3,000 shelters nationwide. Your technology costs are local hardware (scanners, photo stations, tablets) and your own website. Correct the output when the tool gets this wrong; that judgment is your job.

### 3. Budget Narrative (Brief)

Write 1 page explaining your budget rationale:
- Why did you allocate resources this way?
- What are the biggest financial risks in Year 1? (What happens when the Petco Love founding grant tapers? What if registration drives cost more per pet than projected?)
- What assumptions are you making about municipal buy-in and volunteer capacity?
- How does this budget support the strategic goals you'll articulate in Week 9?
