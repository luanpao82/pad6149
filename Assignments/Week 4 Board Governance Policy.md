## What Is This Assignment?

In PAD 6237, you conducted a **Governance Case Study** — you analyzed a real nonprofit's board structure, commended good practices, and made recommendations for improvement. That was about evaluating *someone else's* governance.

Now you're on the other side. You're **designing governance from scratch** for the start-up you're building this semester: the **Petco Love Lost start-up**, a nonprofit jointly created by **Halifax Humane Society** and **Petco Love** to operate the free, AI-powered Petco Love Lost photo-matching database across Volusia County. The question isn't "what's wrong with their board?" — it's "what should *this* board look like, and why?"

This is a real case. Both Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love have agreed to provide information and data to support your work, and after the semester your completed site is shared with both partners — your governance design may inform how the program is actually run in Volusia County. So take the design seriously.

This assignment asks you to take what you learned in PAD 6237 and apply it to this organization. You'll produce a formal Board Governance Policy Document, then publish the key elements as the Leadership page on your website.

## What Do I Submit?

A **5–7 page, double-spaced** Board Governance Policy Document covering:

### 1. Board Mission and Role
- What is the board's purpose within the Petco Love Lost start-up?
- How does the board uphold public service values and the organization's single mission — reuniting lost pets with their families across Volusia County?
- Reference: How does this reflect what you learned about effective governance in PAD 6237?

### 2. Board Composition Policy
- Board size (typically 7–15 for a new nonprofit)
- Term lengths and term limits
- How seats are allocated across the two founding partners and the community served
- Recruitment strategy — where will you find board members with the right expertise?

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Board Composition
> **Board size:** 11 members (expandable to 13)
> **Term:** 3 years, renewable once (6-year maximum)
>
> **Composition priorities** *(one plausible version — your design may differ):*
> - **2 seats appointed by Halifax Humane Society** — reflecting the founding partner that shelters, treats, and rehomes nearly 15,000 animals a year across nine Volusia County municipalities
> - **2 seats appointed by Petco Love** — reflecting the national partner that operates the Petco Love Lost database (3,000+ shelters, 300,000+ pets registered, 100,000+ reunions to date)
> - **At least 2 members drawn from the Volusia County animal-welfare community** — foster/rescue network volunteers, municipal animal-services staff, or residents who have used lost-and-found services
> - **At least 1 member with veterinary or animal-welfare expertise**
> - **At least 1 member with data/technology expertise** — to help the board steward the AI photo-matching platform responsibly (data privacy, accuracy, accessibility)
> - **At least 1 member with fundraising/development experience**
> - **At least 1 member with legal expertise** (nonprofit or technology/data law preferred)
>
> **Recruitment strategy:** A nominating committee identifies candidates through the two founding partners, the Volusia County foster and rescue networks, local veterinary associations, UCF alumni networks, and the local bar association (for legal expertise).
>
> *Note: This composition plan was informed by my PAD 6237 case study, where I found that the organization I analyzed lacked the mix of expertise its work demanded and had no clear rule for balancing competing interests on the board. For a joint venture stewarding a shared technology platform, I designed the composition from the start to (a) balance the two founders with equal appointed seats, (b) guarantee independent community and technical voices so neither founder dominates, and (c) put data/technology expertise on the board because the program's value depends on the photo-matching platform working well.*

### 3. Committee Structure
- Standing committees (e.g., Executive, Finance, Fundraising/Development, Program, Governance/Nominating)
- Consider a **Technology & Data committee** to oversee the photo-matching platform
- Each committee's purpose and membership expectations

### 4. Board Member Responsibilities
- Fiduciary duties (duty of care, duty of loyalty, duty of obedience)
- Give/get policy — financial contribution expectations
- Attendance and participation expectations
- Ambassadorship — representing the start-up across the nine Volusia County municipalities it serves

### 5. Conflict of Interest Policy
- A full written policy (1–2 pages within the document)
- Disclosure requirements
- Procedures for handling conflicts

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Conflict of Interest Excerpt
> **Section 3: Disclosure Requirements**
>
> Each board member shall annually sign a Conflict of Interest Disclosure Statement identifying any relationships, positions, or circumstances that could contribute to a conflict of interest, including but not limited to: (a) employment, consulting, or governance relationships with Halifax Humane Society or Petco Love; (b) family relationships with staff, vendors, or major donors; (c) financial interests in technology vendors, veterinary practices, or other entities that contract with the start-up.
>
> **Section 4: Founder-Appointed Board Service**
> Because two of the start-up's board seats are appointed by Halifax Humane Society and two by Petco Love, the organization acknowledges that these members hold a dual loyalty — to the start-up and to the founding partner that appointed them. This structure is permitted and expected as part of a joint venture. However, founder-appointed members must recuse themselves from any vote on contracts, data-sharing agreements, or funding arrangements between the start-up and the partner that appointed them, and must disclose the relationship annually. No single founder's appointees may constitute a majority of any committee that approves such agreements.

### 6. Meeting Structure and Quorum Rules
- Meeting frequency (quarterly is standard; monthly for start-ups)
- Quorum requirements
- Procedures for remote participation and voting

For Florida-specific governance requirements — including board composition rules, filing obligations, and annual report deadlines — check the **National Council of Nonprofits** (councilofnonprofits.org) state-by-state resource page and the **Florida Division of Corporations** at **Sunbiz.org**. Reviewing actual articles of incorporation filed by similar Florida nonprofits on Sunbiz can help you understand the legal language and structure your governance document should reflect.

## Website Update

Real nonprofit websites don't post their full governance policy documents online. What they do post: a clear, concise Leadership page that tells visitors who governs the organization and how. Your website should work the same way.

**Add a "Leadership" page with:**
- A brief governance philosophy statement (2–3 sentences)
- Board member names and bios (fictional for now — but write them as if they're real). Use **Canva AI** to create a clean board member profile grid with photo placeholders and bios
- Committee structure (a simple list or table)
- A "Download" link to the full Board Governance Policy (upload as PDF or link to a Google Doc)

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Leadership Page Structure
> **Page title:** Our Leadership
>
> **Intro paragraph:**
> "Our start-up is governed by a volunteer Board of Directors that brings together our two founding partners — Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love — alongside independent voices from Volusia County's animal-welfare community. Together they steward a single mission: reuniting lost pets with their families across all nine of our municipalities, using the free Petco Love Lost photo-matching database."
>
> **Board Members:** *(listed with photo placeholder, name, title, 1-sentence bio)*
> - Board Chair — Retired Volusia County animal-services director, 20 years in regional animal welfare
> - Vice Chair (Halifax appointee) — Halifax Humane Society operations leader
> - Vice Chair (Petco Love appointee) — Petco Love national program manager for Petco Love Lost
> - Treasurer — CPA with nonprofit financial management experience
> - Technology Director — Data engineer specializing in image-matching and data privacy
> - *(etc.)*
>
> **Committees:** Executive · Finance · Fundraising/Development · Program · Technology & Data · Governance & Nominating
>
> **Footer:**
> 📄 [Download our full Board Governance Policy (PDF)]

**How to do this efficiently:**
1. Write the full policy document in Word (your graded submission)
2. Save as PDF
3. Upload the PDF to your website (Google Sites: Insert → File; Wix: Add → Document)
4. Write the summary content directly on the web page (~15 minutes)
5. Link the PDF with a download button

This is one deliverable in two formats — the academic version (Word, submitted to Webcourses) and the professional version (web summary + PDF download, on your site). This is exactly how you'd do it for a real organization — which this one may become.
