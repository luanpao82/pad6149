## What Is This Assignment?

Nonprofits don't just deliver services — they **advocate for the cause they serve**. A single organization can operate the Petco Love Lost database well and still watch thousands of pets cycle through shelters because the *rules* around lost-and-found animals haven't caught up. Policy change is how you address root causes, not just symptoms.

In PAD 6142, you developed a Policy Advocacy Plan for a nonprofit organization — identifying a policy issue, mapping the political landscape, and designing an advocacy strategy within 501(c)(3) legal boundaries. Now you're doing the same for the start-up you're building this semester: the **Petco Love Lost start-up**, a nonprofit jointly created by **Halifax Humane Society** and **Petco Love** to operate the free, AI-powered Petco Love Lost photo-matching database across Volusia County. The question is: what policy change would most improve pet reunification in Volusia County, and what can your nonprofit legally and strategically do about it?

This is a real case. Both Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love have agreed to provide information and data to support your work, and after the semester your completed site is shared with both partners — your advocacy plan may inform how the program actually engages with Volusia County government. So make it real and make it usable.

This assignment is also a test of a critical nonprofit leadership skill: understanding **what you can and cannot do** as a tax-exempt organization. 501(c)(3)s can educate, research, and advocate — but they cannot endorse candidates or spend a "substantial part" of their activities on lobbying. Knowing where the line is matters.

## What Do I Submit?

### 1. Policy Issue Identification (1 page)

Identify **one specific policy issue** that directly affects lost-and-found pet outcomes in Volusia County. Be precise — not "help lost pets" but a concrete, jurisdiction-anchored issue. Plausible angles for this case:

- **Mandatory scan-for-owner ordinance** — requiring shelters, animal-services officers, and veterinary clinics to scan every found animal for a microchip *and* run its photo through a lost-and-found database (like Petco Love Lost) before intake or transfer.
- **Microchipping policy** — county incentives, low-cost chip clinics, or a chip-at-adoption requirement.
- **County animal-services funding** — dedicated funding for field scanning, database integration, and return-to-owner (RTO) staffing.
- **Shelter intake / return-to-owner policy** — a mandatory hold-and-search step before an animal enters the shelter population.
- **Data-sharing among Volusia County shelters** — a countywide agreement so all nine municipalities and area rescues search one shared lost-and-found pool.

For your chosen issue, address:
- What is the current policy (or lack of policy)?
- Who is affected, and how? (families, the animals, the shelter, taxpayers)
- What level of government has jurisdiction? (local, state, federal)
- What is the current political landscape around this issue?

Use **Perplexity.ai** to research the current status: *"What are Florida's current laws on microchipping and scanning found/stray animals for owners? Do any Florida counties or cities require shelters to scan for microchips or search a lost-and-found database before intake? What has been proposed 2024–2026?"*

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Policy Issue
> **Issue:** Volusia County has no countywide requirement that found or stray animals be scanned for a microchip *and* checked against a lost-and-found photo database before they enter the shelter system.
>
> **Current situation:** Florida law addresses microchip *registration* and stray *hold periods*, but there is no Volusia County ordinance mandating a scan-and-search step at the point a found animal is picked up. Practice is inconsistent across the nine municipalities and the area's rescues — some scan reliably, some don't, and few systematically run a found animal's photo through a national database like Petco Love Lost. As a result, animals that already have a home enter intake, occupy kennel space, and sometimes get adopted out or worse before their family is ever contacted.
>
> **Who is affected:** Roughly **10 million dogs and cats go missing nationwide each year**, and Halifax Humane Society alone shelters, treats, and rehomes **nearly 15,000 animals a year** across nine Volusia County municipalities. Every found pet that could have been reunited at the curb — as Halifax's CEO put it, *"can help reunite lost pets within minutes — without them ever having to enter a shelter"* — instead consumes intake staff time, kennel space, and county dollars.
>
> **Jurisdiction:** County and municipal level — the Volusia County Council and the nine municipal governments set animal-services ordinances and fund the shelter contract. (Some elements, such as microchip-registry standards, sit at the state level.)
>
> **Political landscape:** Animal welfare is a low-controversy, high-sympathy issue in Volusia County, and a scan-for-owner rule is framed as *fiscally responsible* (fewer animals in intake = lower shelter cost) as well as humane. The obstacles are cost and mandate fatigue among municipalities and small rescues, not ideological opposition. Petco Love Lost is free, which neutralizes much of the cost objection.

### 2. Advocacy Goals and Objectives (half a page)

Define what your organization is trying to achieve through advocacy. Use SMART criteria:
- **Goal:** The broad policy change you seek
- **Objectives:** Specific, measurable steps toward that goal

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Advocacy Goals
> **Goal:** Establish a countywide scan-for-owner standard so that every found or stray animal in Volusia County is scanned for a microchip and searched against the Petco Love Lost database before entering the shelter system.
>
> **Objectives:**
> 1. By March 2027, present a reunification-gap report to the Volusia County Council documenting how many intake animals were already microchipped or could have been matched at the curb
> 2. By June 2027, secure written commitments from at least 3 of the 9 municipalities (and Halifax's animal-services operation) to adopt a scan-and-search protocol at pickup
> 3. By December 2027, contribute to the drafting and introduction of a countywide scan-for-owner ordinance or an interlocal data-sharing agreement

### 3. 501(c)(3) Legal Framework (half a page)

Demonstrate that you understand what your organization can and cannot do:

| Activity | Permitted? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Voter education** (nonpartisan) | ✅ Yes | Candidate forums, voter guides (must be nonpartisan) |
| **Issue advocacy** | ✅ Yes | Educating the public and policymakers about your issue — no limits |
| **Direct lobbying** | ⚠️ Limited | Contacting officials about specific legislation — permitted but cannot be a "substantial part" of activities |
| **Grassroots lobbying** | ⚠️ Limited | Asking the public to contact officials about specific measures — same limits apply |
| **Candidate endorsement** | ❌ No | Absolutely prohibited for 501(c)(3)s |
| **Campaign contributions** | ❌ No | Absolutely prohibited |

Use **ChatGPT or Claude** to help clarify: *"Explain the IRS rules on 501(c)(3) lobbying limits. What is the 'substantial part' test vs. the 501(h) election? Which is better for a small start-up nonprofit?"*

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Legal Framework
> The start-up will operate under the **501(h) election**, which provides clear expenditure limits for lobbying rather than the vague "substantial part" test. Under 501(h), an organization with a budget under $500,000 may spend up to 20% of its exempt-purpose expenditures on lobbying — well above anything a lean start-up would spend advocating for a county scan-for-owner ordinance.
>
> The majority of the start-up's advocacy work — publishing reunification-gap data, educating the public and municipal staff, convening area shelters around data-sharing — qualifies as **issue advocacy**, which has no spending limits under IRS rules. One added care point for this case: because the start-up is a joint venture of Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, any advocacy positions are coordinated with both founders so the organization doesn't advocate against a partner's interests.

### 4. Advocacy Strategy (2–3 pages)

Design your advocacy approach using multiple tactics. For each tactic, explain what you'll do, who is responsible, and what resources are needed.

#### Community Education and Research
- How will you educate the public and municipal decision-makers about the reunification gap?
- What data or research will you produce? (e.g., how many intake animals were already chipped; how many curbside matches Petco Love Lost enabled)
- Use **Google Scholar** to find research on microchip return-to-owner rates, shelter intake reduction, and field-scanning outcomes

#### Coalition Building
- What organizations will you partner with? (the nine municipalities' animal-services units, area rescues and fosters, veterinary clinics, Volusia County Council staff)
- How will a coalition amplify your impact beyond what your start-up could do alone?
- Use **Candid / GuideStar** (candid.org) to identify animal-welfare organizations and funders active in the region

#### Policymaker Engagement
- Who are the specific policymakers you need to reach? (Name them — Volusia County Council members, relevant municipal officials)
- How will you build relationships? (meetings, testimony, policy briefs, invitations to watch a curbside reunification in action)
- What is your "ask" — the specific action you want each policymaker to take?

#### Public Awareness
- How will you bring public attention to lost-and-found pets? (a lost pet is a naturally shareable, sympathetic story)
- What channels will you use? (social media, op-eds, local news, community events)
- Use **Canva** to design an advocacy one-pager or infographic — e.g., "One scan can send a pet home before it ever enters a shelter."

#### Community Mobilization
- How will you engage the people most affected — pet owners who have lost or found an animal?
- How will you make it easy to take action? (register your pet's photo, sign the petition, give public comment, attend a Council meeting)

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Advocacy Strategy (Excerpt)
> **Policymaker Engagement:**
> - Schedule meetings with Volusia County Council members [Name] and [Name], plus animal-services leads in the two or three municipalities with the highest intake numbers
> - Present the reunification-gap data during a County Council public-comment session
> - Invite Council members to observe a curbside scan-and-match in the field — let them see a pet go home *"within minutes — without ever having to enter a shelter"*
> - Deliver a one-page policy brief (designed in **Canva**) summarizing the issue, the data, and the proposed scan-for-owner standard
>
> **Coalition & Community Mobilization:**
> - Convene the nine municipalities' animal-services units, area rescues, and local veterinary clinics into a **Volusia County Reunification Coalition** around a shared data-sharing protocol
> - Host a community event where residents register their pets' photos in Petco Love Lost and hear reunion stories
> - Create a simple online petition via **Google Forms** — collect signatures supporting a countywide scan-for-owner ordinance
> - Recruit and prep 8–10 residents (including a few who were reunited with a pet) to give 2-minute testimony at a County Council meeting

How will you measure advocacy success? Define:

- Long-term metrics (ordinance passed, curbside reunifications, reduction in intake of already-owned animals, higher return-to-owner rate)
