## What Is This Assignment?

You have a budget (Week 7). Now the harder question: **where does the money actually come from, and how do you get it?**

You submit your strategy document to Webcourses. Your fundraising strategy feeds directly into the **Strategic Plan (Week 9, 7 pts)**, so build it with that level of seriousness.

In PAD 5146, you produced a comprehensive Fundraising Plan — including a fundraising audit, SWOT analysis, donor cultivation strategies, and an ethics and stewardship narrative. That was for an established organization. Now you're designing a fundraising strategy for a **brand-new start-up** with no donor history of its own, no track record, and a name the public hasn't heard yet. The challenge is fundamentally different.

> [!info] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up
> Your organization is the start-up jointly created by **Halifax Humane Society** and **Petco Love** to run the **Petco Love Lost** program — a free, AI-powered photo-matching database that reunites lost pets with their families — across **Volusia County, Florida** (Daytona Beach and nine municipalities). The technology and the reunions are the mission; your job is to fund the **local operation**: outreach, staffing, microchip and registration clinics, and community education that get pets into the database *before* they go missing and get "found" photos uploaded fast when they do.
>
> Fundraising here has a distinctive shape. You have a powerful national partner (Petco Love) and a credible local anchor (Halifax, which shelters nearly 15,000 animals a year), but you cannot simply lean on either forever. And you have something most start-ups would kill for: **an endless supply of real reunion stories.** Design a strategy that turns those stories into sustainable local revenue.

## What Do I Submit?

### 1. Fundraising SWOT Analysis

Before designing your strategy, assess your starting position honestly.

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** — What assets do you have? (partner credibility, shelter reach, a database that already produces results, reunion stories) | **Weaknesses** — What's missing? (no independent donor database, unknown local brand, no development staff, over-reliance on one partner) |
| **External** | **Opportunities** — What's in your favor? (pet ownership is widespread and emotional, animal-welfare funders are active, local vets and pet businesses are natural allies) | **Threats** — What could block you? (competition with the shelter's own fundraising, donor fatigue, economic downturn, "isn't Petco already paying for this?" confusion) |

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Fundraising SWOT
> | | Helpful | Harmful |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Internal** | Backed by two trusted names (Petco Love nationally, Halifax Humane Society locally); the Petco Love Lost database already has a track record — 300,000+ pets registered and 100,000+ reunions nationwide; a steady stream of authentic, emotional reunion stories to tell | No donor list of our own; the start-up's local brand is brand-new; no dedicated development staff in Year 1; risk of being seen as "just a Petco program" rather than a nonprofit that needs support |
> | **External** | Millions of households own pets and roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing every year — a problem donors instantly understand; national animal-welfare funders (PetSmart Charities, Maddie's Fund, Bissell Pet Foundation) actively grant to reunification work; local vet clinics, groomers, and pet retailers in Volusia County are eager, low-cost partners | Competing for the same Volusia County animal-lover donors that Halifax already solicits; individual giving softens in a downturn; public assumption that a corporate partner fully funds the work |
>
> *This SWOT sharpened my strategy: our single biggest fundraising asset is reunion storytelling, and our single biggest risk is dependence on our founding partners — so Year 1 has to start building an independent local donor base.*

### 2. Revenue Strategy by Source

For each revenue source in your Week 7 budget, develop a specific plan for how you'll actually secure that funding. Don't just list amounts — describe the **who, what, and how**.

#### Petco Love Partnership & Foundation Grants

Your founding partner, **Petco Love**, is your anchor funder — but treat it as a **launchpad, not a permanent subsidy.** Clarify what Petco Love provides (seed support, in-kind access to the Love Lost platform, possible matching or challenge gifts) and then build a diversified grant pipeline *around* it.

- Identify **3–5 specific funders** beyond Petco Love that support pet reunification, microchipping, and shelter-diversion work. Use **Candid's Foundation Directory** (free through UCF Library databases) and **Candid's free 990 search** (candid.org) to find who funds comparable animal-welfare organizations.
- For each, note: name, typical grant size, focus area, and application timeline.
- Which grants will you apply for in Year 1? Be realistic — most foundations run 6–12 month cycles.

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Grant Prospects (one plausible version)
> | Funder | Typical Grant Size | Focus Area | Timeline |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Petco Love (founding partner) | Seed + matching | Pet reunification, Love Lost platform, lifesaving grants | Ongoing partner relationship |
> | PetSmart Charities | $10,000–$100,000 | Keeping pets with families; shelter diversion | Rolling / annual cycles |
> | Maddie's Fund | $5,000–$50,000 | Community lifesaving, data-driven animal welfare | Program-specific cycles |
> | Bissell Pet Foundation | $5,000–$25,000 | Microchipping, reunification, shelter support | Rolling applications |
> | Community Foundation of Central Florida | $5,000–$25,000 | Local community services in the region | March & September cycles |
>
> *Year 1 target: Petco Love seed/match plus 2 additional grants totaling roughly $70,000. Realistic because reunification is exactly what these animal-welfare funders prioritize, and a Petco Love match makes our proposals more competitive.*

#### Individual Donations — Pet Owners

Your most natural donors are **pet owners** across Volusia County: people whose own dog or cat is a family member, and who can picture the panic of a missing pet.

- Who are your first donors? (Board members, Halifax's existing adopter and volunteer network — with permission, veterinary-clinic clients, and families who have *already been reunited* through the database.)
- What is your **give/get policy** for board members?
- How will you build a donor pipeline from zero? Use **Perplexity.ai** to research best practices for converting animal-lover audiences into recurring donors.

#### Local Businesses & Veterinary Clinics

Volusia County's **veterinary clinics, groomers, doggy daycares, and pet-supply stores** are your highest-yield local partners — both as sponsors and as places to reach pet owners.

- Identify 3–5 local businesses and clinics that might sponsor you or host registration drives.
- What do you offer in return? (Logo on the site, "Love Lost partner" window decal, social-media features, co-branded microchip clinics, community goodwill.)
- Vet clinics are special: they can **sponsor**, **host registration events**, *and* **refer clients** into the database. Research local giving with **Perplexity.ai**: *"Which veterinary clinics, pet-supply retailers, and animal-related businesses operate in Volusia County / Daytona Beach, Florida?"*

#### Reunion-Story-Driven Appeals & Events

You have a fundraising asset most nonprofits envy: **real reunion stories.** Every reunion is a ready-made appeal.

- Plan how you'll capture and share reunion stories (with owner consent) as the emotional core of your campaigns — end-of-year appeal, Giving Tuesday, "reunited this year" recap.
- Plan one fundraising event for Year 1 — for example a "Lost & Found Reunion Day" or a microchip-clinic fundraiser co-hosted with a local vet.
- Estimate gross vs. net revenue honestly (events are expensive). Use **Canva** to design a mock event flyer or a reunion-story social graphic.

#### Government Grants and Contracts

- Are there county or municipal animal-services funds, or state animal-welfare grants, that support reunification and shelter-diversion work in Volusia County?
- Search **Grants.gov** (grants.gov) and the Volusia County / municipal government sites for local RFPs.
- Be honest: most government funding requires an established track record. What's your realistic path to eligibility — perhaps *through* Halifax's existing county relationships?

### 3. Donor Communications Plan

How will you communicate with donors and prospects?

- **Email newsletter** — What platform will you use? (Mailchimp offers a free tier for up to 500 contacts.) How often? What content? (Reunion stories are your best open-rate driver.)
- **Social media** — Which platforms? What's your posting strategy? (Lost-and-found pet content is highly shareable — lean into it.)
- **Annual report** — Even in Year 1, plan a simple impact report: pets registered, reunions made, families served.
- **Thank-you process** — How quickly will you acknowledge gifts? Through what channels?

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Donor Communications
> - **Email:** Monthly newsletter via **Mailchimp** (free tier) — one featured reunion story, registration reminders, and upcoming clinic dates
> - **Social media:** Instagram + Facebook as primary (reunion photos and "found this pet — do you know them?" posts are highly shareable in local groups); LinkedIn for vet-clinic and business sponsors. Use **Canva** for templated, on-brand reunion-story graphics
> - **Annual report:** Simple 4-page PDF in **Canva** — "In our first year: X pets registered, Y reunions, Z families reunited" — emailed and posted on the website by December
> - **Thank-you:** Personalized email within 48 hours of any gift; a reunion-photo thank-you card for gifts over $100; a call from the board chair for gifts over $500

### 4. Ethics and Stewardship

Draw on the ethics and stewardship narrative from your PAD 5146 plan, and add the wrinkles this case creates:

- What are your ethical commitments around fundraising?
- **Story consent:** reunion stories involve real families and their pets. How will you obtain permission before using names, photos, or details in appeals?
- **Partner clarity:** how will you honestly represent the roles of Petco Love and Halifax so donors understand what their gift adds *beyond* what the partners already provide?
- What gifts would you decline, and why?
- How will you maintain transparency about how funds are used?

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Ethics Statement
> Our start-up commits to ethical fundraising aligned with the Association of Fundraising Professionals (AFP) Code of Ethics. Specifically:
> - We obtain **written consent** from any family before featuring their reunion story, name, or pet's photo in fundraising materials, and we let them withdraw it at any time
> - We represent our founding partners honestly: donors are told that Petco Love provides the free matching platform and seed support, and that local gifts fund the **county operation** — outreach, clinics, and staffing — that Petco Love does not cover
> - All donor-restricted gifts are used exclusively for their stated purpose
> - We publish annual financial and impact summaries on our website's Transparency page
> - We do not share donor information with third parties without explicit consent
>
> *This framework was informed by my PAD 5146 analysis, where the organization I studied lacked a clear gift-acceptance policy — an especially important gap here, where our appeals depend on the trust of the very families we serve.*

## Website Update

Add a **"Donate" or "Support Us"** page to your website. This page should make it easy for someone who's ready to give — and compelling for someone who's still deciding.

**Your page should include:**
- A clear statement of why donations matter — connect giving to **reunions**, not to your budget gap ("your gift keeps a family from ever losing their pet")
- Giving levels with descriptions of what each level supports (e.g., "$50 sponsors a free microchip-and-register visit for one pet")
- A call to action — even without a real payment processor, include a "Donate" button or a "Contact us to give" prompt
- Information about other ways to support: volunteering at clinics, vet-clinic and business sponsorships, in-kind donations (microchips, scanner equipment)
- Use **Canva** to design a visual giving-level graphic or a reunion-impact infographic

> [!example] The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Donate Page
> **Page title:** Help Us Bring Them Home
>
> **Intro:** "About 10 million dogs and cats go missing in the U.S. every year — and in Volusia County, too many end up in a shelter cage instead of back on the couch. Our free photo-matching database can reunite a lost pet with its family in minutes, without the pet ever entering a shelter. Your gift funds the local outreach, clinics, and staff that make those reunions happen."
>
> **Giving Levels:**
> - **$25 — First Alert:** Helps us register lost-and-found pets and push "found pet" alerts across the county
> - **$50 — Chip & Check:** Sponsors a free microchip-and-register visit for one pet at a community clinic
> - **$150 — Reunion Maker:** Powers a weekend registration drive at a local vet clinic or pet store
> - **$500 — Homeward Circle:** Funds a full countywide "Is this your pet?" outreach push
> - **$1,000 — Founding Friend:** Become a founding donor — your name on our first annual report and an invitation to our Reunion Day celebration
>
> **Other Ways to Give:** Business & vet-clinic sponsorships · In-kind microchips and scanners · Volunteer at a clinic · Share a reunion story
>
