## What Is This Assignment?

This week you build the digital home for the **Petco Love Lost start-up** — the shared case this whole track is built around. By the end of the week, the organization should have a **live, publicly accessible website** — even if it's minimal.

Here's the case in one breath. Roughly **10 million dogs and cats go missing in the United States every year.** Petco Love Lost is a **free, AI-powered photo-matching database**: you upload a photo of a lost or found pet, and it searches a national pool in seconds. It's now used by **more than 3,000 shelters and rescues**, with **over 300,000 pets registered** and **more than 100,000 reunions to date.** In Volusia County, **Halifax Humane Society** — a Daytona Beach nonprofit that shelters, treats, and rehomes **nearly 15,000 animals a year** across **nine municipalities** — has partnered with **Petco Love** to bring Petco Love Lost to the area in the first coordinated countywide deployment. Your job this semester is to imagine that partnership as its own **start-up nonprofit**, jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, whose single purpose is to run Petco Love Lost well in Volusia County — and to build out its website one deliverable at a time.

> [!info] You don't name it yet
> You'll finalize the organization's name and brand in **Week 6 (Confirming Your Identity)**. Until then, use a clear **working name** everywhere on the site — for example, **"[your start-up name — finalized in Week 6]"** or a plain placeholder like *"Volusia Pet Reunion Project (working name)."* This lets you launch now without locking in branding you'll refine later.

This isn't a web design class, and you don't need any coding experience. You'll use a free website builder and AI tools to generate content and visuals. The site starts simple and grows throughout the semester as you complete each deliverable.

Why a website? Because in 2026, a nonprofit without a web presence doesn't exist. Funders check your site before writing a check. Partners check it before returning your call. Your website is the organization's first impression — and for this course, it's also **the portfolio that demonstrates your capstone work**. Remember: after the semester, every completed site is **shared with Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love**, and your work **may inform how the program is actually operated** in Volusia County. This is not a hypothetical that ends with a grade.

## Choosing a Website Builder

You need a free platform that lets you publish a multi-page site without coding. Here are the options:

| Platform | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google Sites** (sites.google.com) | Fastest setup | Free, no ads, integrates with Google Workspace | Limited design flexibility |
| **Carrd.co** | Clean one-page sites | Beautiful templates, very simple | Free tier is one page only |
| **Wix.com** | Design flexibility | Drag-and-drop, lots of templates | Free tier shows Wix branding |
| **WordPress.com** | Long-term scalability | Most powerful, huge template library | Steeper learning curve |
| **GitHub Pages** | Technical users | Full control, professional URL | Requires basic HTML |

**Recommendation:** If you want the fastest path to a live site, use **Google Sites**. If you care about aesthetics and want something portfolio-worthy, consider **Wix** or **WordPress.com**.

## Step-by-Step: Building Your Site with Google Sites

If you're using Google Sites (recommended for speed), here's the workflow:

**Step 1:** Go to [sites.google.com](https://sites.google.com) → Click the **+** button to create a blank site

**Step 2:** Enter your working organization name as the site title

**Step 3:** Add a hero banner — upload an image or use a stock photo. For free, high-quality stock photos, try **Unsplash** (unsplash.com) or **Pexels** (pexels.com) — both are free for commercial use. For a custom image, use Canva:
- Go to [canva.com](https://canva.com) → search "website banner"
- Use Magic Design or text-to-image with a prompt like: *"A happy dog reunited with its family on a Florida porch, warm natural light, hopeful and professional nonprofit tone"*

**Step 4:** Add your mission statement and About Us text below the banner

**Step 5:** Create additional pages using the **Pages** panel on the right:
- Click **+** → Add page → Name each one (Leadership, Team, Get Involved, Financials, Donate, Strategy, Programs, Our Approach, Advocacy)
- Add a placeholder line: *"This page is under development. Check back as our organization grows."*

**Step 6:** Click **Publish** → Choose a URL (e.g., `sites.google.com/view/volusiapetreunion`)

**Step 7 (post-publish):** Run your live URL through **WAVE** (wave.webaim.org) — a free accessibility checker that flags missing alt text, low contrast, and heading structure issues. Fix what it flags. Then add a **favicon** using **Favicon.io** (favicon.io) — generate a small icon from your org's initials so the browser tab looks polished. Finally, paste your web copy into the **Hemingway Editor** (hemingwayapp.com) to check readability — web visitors scan, so aim for clear, concise prose at a Grade 8–10 reading level.

Total time: 30–60 minutes (plus 15 minutes for the polish steps).

## Creating Your Visual Identity

Your site needs a basic brand identity. You don't need a graphic designer — AI tools handle this well. Keep it **light** for now: you'll confirm the real name, logo, and palette in **Week 6**, so treat everything this week as a placeholder you're free to change.

### Logo
> [!tip] Creating a Logo with Canva AI
> Go to [canva.com](https://canva.com) → Search "logo" → Select a template or use **Magic Design**:
> ```
> Create a professional logo for a start-up nonprofit that reunites
> lost and found pets with their families in Volusia County, Florida,
> using a free AI photo-matching database. Working name: [your name].
> Style: clean, modern, trustworthy, warm. Imagery: consider a pet
> silhouette, a heart, or a "home" motif. Colors: [your preferences].
> ```
> Download as PNG with transparent background.

### Brand Colors
Choose 2–3 colors that reflect your organization's tone:
- A **primary color** (dominant — headers, buttons)
- A **secondary color** (accents)
- A **neutral** (background, body text)

Not sure where to start? **Coolors.co** generates harmonious color palettes — enter one color you like, lock it, and keep generating until you find a combination that fits your organization's tone. For typography, browse **Google Fonts** (fonts.google.com) to find a professional font pairing — one for headings, one for body text.
