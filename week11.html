What Is This Assignment?

Nonprofit leaders don't operate in a vacuum. The challenges your organization faces — rising demand, strained shelter capacity, workforce shortages, shifting community expectations, uncertain funding — are playing out in real time, in the news, in your community. The question is: does your organization understand these forces, and does it have a plan to navigate them?

For the Petco Love Lost start-up, the issue is lost and found pets. Roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing in the United States each year. When they end up in overcrowded shelters instead of back home, the cost is borne by families, by taxpayers, and by the animals themselves. Petco Love Lost — a free, AI-powered photo-matching database now used by more than 3,000 shelters and rescues, with over 300,000 pets registered and more than 100,000 reunions to date — was built to close that reunification gap. Your start-up, jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, exists to operate that program well across Volusia County, Florida.

This assignment asks you to find and analyze 3–5 recent news articles about the lost-and-found-pet issue and the shelter landscape, connect what the media is reporting to the reality on the ground in Volusia County, and translate that analysis into an "Our Approach" page on your website.

This is not a current events report. It's a leadership exercise: reading the landscape, identifying patterns, finding the gaps, and articulating how your organization responds. Funders, partners, and board members expect nonprofit leaders to be fluent in the public discourse around their issue — and to have a point of view.

This is the single largest assignment in the course (30 pts) and the one requirement shared by both capstone paths. It requires analytical depth — not summary.

The Case

This week continues the shared Option B case. You are leading a start-up nonprofit, jointly created by Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love, whose single purpose is to operate the Petco Love Lost program well in Volusia County. The partners, the problem, and the data are real; the organization is the one you have been designing since Week 3. Both Halifax Humane Society and Petco Love have agreed to share information to support your work, and completed sites may inform how the program is actually operated in the county.

Source coursework: PAD 6149 (this course) — you will draw on your Strategic Plan (Week 9), Program Evaluation (Week 10), and earlier deliverables to ground your leadership analysis.

Step 1: Find Your Articles

Select 3–5 news articles published within the past 2 years (2024–2026) that are directly related to the lost-and-found-pet issue and the shelter landscape your organization addresses.

Article selection criteria:

Requirement Details
Recency Published 2024–2026
Quantity Minimum 3, maximum 5
Source quality Published journalism — newspapers, news websites, or sector media (Nonprofit Quarterly, SSIR, Chronicle of Philanthropy, animal-welfare trade press). No blogs, social media posts, or opinion columns without editorial oversight
Geographic mix At least 1 local/regional article (Volusia County, Daytona Beach, or Florida) + at least 1 national article

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Article Selection

  1. Local: "Volusia County shelters strain as intake outpaces adoptions" — Daytona Beach News-Journal, April 2025. Reporting on capacity pressure across the county's municipal and nonprofit shelters.
  2. Local/Regional: "Halifax Humane Society brings AI photo-matching to reunite lost pets across Volusia" — WESH 2 Orlando, September 2025. Coverage of the first coordinated countywide deployment of Petco Love Lost.
  3. National: "As shelters overflow, tech promises to get lost pets home faster" — Nonprofit Quarterly, February 2025. Analysis of reunification technology and its adoption by shelters and rescues.
  4. National: "Ten million pets go missing every year. Why so few make it home." — Stanford Social Innovation Review, Fall 2024. Research-informed analysis of the reunification gap.
  5. State: "Florida animal services face rising intake and shrinking budgets" — Tampa Bay Times, June 2025. Statewide look at funding and staffing pressures on animal control and shelters.

These five articles cover different dimensions: shelter capacity, reunification technology, the national reunification gap, and funding/staffing pressures. Together, they paint a picture of the landscape the Petco Love Lost start-up operates in.

Part I: Essay (2,500–3,000 words)

Analyze your articles — not one by one, but as a body of evidence about the lost-and-found-pet issue your organization addresses. Your essay should address five questions:

Question 1: What is happening?

What picture do your articles paint of the lost-and-found-pet issue and the shelter landscape? Read across the articles for patterns: what themes keep appearing? What communities and shelters are most affected? What's getting worse, staying the same, or improving?

This is pattern analysis, not summary. Don't write "Article 1 says X, Article 2 says Y." Instead: "Across five recent reports, three recurring themes emerge..."

Use local data from Census Reporter (censusreporter.org) and Social Explorer (UCF Library), plus shelter intake and outcome data where available, to add quantitative context to the stories the articles tell.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up

"Across five articles spanning 2024–2025, three patterns emerge in the landscape of lost and found pets. First, shelter overcrowding is structural, not seasonal — intake is outpacing adoptions in Volusia County and across Florida, and animals that could go straight home instead occupy scarce kennel space. Second, the reunification gap is enormous: of the roughly 10 million dogs and cats that go missing nationally each year, only a fraction are ever reunited, and most are never registered anywhere a finder can search. Third, technology is emerging as a response — free, AI-powered photo-matching tools like Petco Love Lost, now used by more than 3,000 shelters and rescues, can identify a match in seconds — but coverage depends on coordinated local adoption, which most communities still lack."

Question 2: Why is this happening?

What are the root causes of the challenges your articles describe? Are they primarily economic, operational, technological, behavioral, or structural? Do the national articles and local articles point to the same causes, or does Volusia County have unique factors (geography, tourism-season strays, the number of municipalities served)?

This is the analytical core of your essay. Integrate concepts from your MNM coursework — apply frameworks from PAD 6208 (financial management), PAD 6335 (strategic planning), PAD 6417 (HR management), PAD 6327 (program evaluation), PAD 6142 (nonprofit organizations), or other courses to analyze what's driving the problems.

Use Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) to find academic research — on shelter intake dynamics, return-to-owner rates, or technology adoption in the social sector — that deepens your analysis beyond what the news articles provide.

Question 3: Who is responding?

Identify at least two organizations in your community (nonprofits, funders, government agencies) that are addressing the challenges your articles describe. For each: - What strategies are they using? - Where are they succeeding? - Where are the gaps — what are they not doing or unable to do?

Halifax Humane Society and Volusia County's municipal animal-services and shelter operations are obvious starting points, but push further — regional rescues, veterinary networks, and funders like Petco Love all play a role. Use Candid / GuideStar (candid.org) to research their financials and programs. Use Perplexity.ai to find additional coverage of their work.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Stakeholder Snapshot

"Halifax Humane Society, the Daytona Beach nonprofit that shelters, treats, and rehomes nearly 15,000 animals a year across nine Volusia County municipalities, is the anchor responder. Its strength is reach and credibility; its constraint is that reunification competes with intake, medical care, and adoption for finite staff time. Volusia County animal services and the municipal shelters handle stray intake and enforcement, but their systems are not built to match a found pet to an owner searching online. Petco Love, the national nonprofit that operates the Petco Love Lost database, supplies the technology and funding but not local operations. The gap between the technology and the ground is exactly where the start-up sits."

Question 4: What's missing?

This is the most important question. Based on your analysis of the articles and the organizations responding: what gap remains that your organization exists to fill?

The articles reveal what the media sees. Your job is to identify what the media — and the existing organizations — are not seeing. This is your organization's reason for being, grounded in evidence.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up

"What none of these articles fully address is the coordination gap. Petco Love Lost is free and powerful, but a photo-matching database only works when finders, shelters, veterinary clinics, and owners across a whole county are actually using it — consistently, at the moment of intake, and in the first hours a pet goes missing. Halifax Humane Society has the reach; Petco Love has the tool; Volusia County's municipalities have the strays. No single existing entity is responsible for stitching these together into one coordinated countywide reunification system. As Halifax's CEO put it, the technology 'can help reunite lost pets within minutes — without them ever having to enter a shelter.' The Petco Love Lost start-up exists precisely to make that promise real for every one of the county's nine municipalities — not as a side project of an already-stretched shelter, but as a dedicated operation."

Question 5: How will you lead through this?

Using your analysis, describe how you, as the Executive Director of the Petco Love Lost start-up, would demonstrate innovative leadership to navigate the challenges your articles reveal.

Connect this directly to your Strategic Plan (Week 9) and Program Evaluation design (Week 10). What specific strategies from your plan are designed to address these realities — coordinating across nine municipalities, driving registration at the moment of intake, measuring reunification rates? Where might your plan need to be adjusted based on what you've learned from your media analysis?

This is not aspirational — it's operational. What will you actually do?

Citations: Cite all articles and other sources in APA 7th edition format. Include in-text citations throughout the essay and a full reference list at the end. The reference list does not count toward the word limit.

Website Update: "Our Approach" Page

The essay is your academic analysis. The website page is its public-facing translation. Add an "Our Approach" page that tells visitors: we understand the landscape, we know the challenges, and here's how we work.

Your page should include four sections:

1. "The Issue" — 2–3 sentences summarizing the lost-and-found-pet problem your organization exists to solve, grounded in data from your articles and local research. This is not your mission statement — it's the context for your mission.

2. "What's Missing" — 2–3 sentences identifying the gap that your organization fills. This comes directly from your Q4 analysis — the coordination the articles and existing organizations don't address.

3. "How We Work" — 3–4 organizational principles that define your approach. These should emerge from your Q5 leadership analysis — they're your answer to the challenges you've identified.

4. "Our Partners" — List the organizations you analyzed in Q3, with a brief description of how you collaborate. Use Canva to create a simple partner logo grid or visual.

The Case: Petco Love Lost Start-Up — Our Approach Page

Page title: Our Approach

The Issue: "Roughly 10 million dogs and cats go missing in the United States every year, and most never make it home. In Volusia County, shelter intake is outpacing adoptions, and animals that could be reunited with their families in hours instead occupy scarce kennel space for days or weeks. The technology to close this gap already exists — it just has to reach every corner of the county."

What's Missing: "Petco Love Lost is a free, AI-powered photo-matching database used by more than 3,000 shelters and rescues nationwide. But a tool only reunites pets when everyone uses it — at every shelter, every clinic, and every kitchen table across all nine of Volusia County's municipalities. No one had made that coordination their full-time job. We do."

How We Work: - Reunion before intake — Our first goal is to get a found pet home before it ever enters a shelter, using photo-matching in the first hours a pet goes missing - One county, one system — We coordinate across all nine municipalities so a lost pet in one town can be matched to a finder in the next - Technology in service of people — The AI does the matching; our job is to put it in the hands of finders, owners, clinics, and shelter staff and make it effortless to use - Measured by reunions — Every effort is tracked against one outcome that matters: pets reunited with their families

Our Partners: - Halifax Humane Society — Co-founder; shelter operations, animal care, and countywide credibility - Petco Love — Co-founder; the Petco Love Lost technology platform and national funding - Volusia County animal services & municipal shelters — Stray intake, enforcement, and point-of-intake registration
